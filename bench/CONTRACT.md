## Deliverables and how they are checked

Your work is checked by an automated harness and then judged by people, blind, side by side with other
submissions. The harness only knows your project through the interface below, so follow it exactly.

### 1. `bench.json` at the root of `/workspace`

```json
{
  "build": "npm run build",
  "film": "film/index.html",
  "play": "play/index.html",
  "puzzles": "puzzles.json",
  "sound": "npm run sound",
  "soundFile": "out/sound.wav"
}
```

- `build`: a shell command, run from the project root after `npm install`, that produces the two pages.
- `film` and `play`: paths to the two pages, relative to the project root. They are served over HTTP from the project
  root, so relative URLs work.
- `puzzles`: path to a JSON array of your puzzles (format below).
- `sound` and `soundFile`: a command that writes your soundtrack, and the WAV file it writes.

The values above are an example; use your own paths.

### 2. The film page

When the page is opened with `?record` in its URL, it must expose:

- `window.__ready = true` once everything is built and the first frame can be drawn.
- `window.__duration`: the film's length in seconds.
- `window.__frame(t)`: an async function that draws the film at time `t` seconds and resolves once that frame is on
  screen. The same `t` must always give the same picture: no `Math.random()` without a seed, and no real clock.
- `window.__stats`: optional, an object of numbers describing your scene (for example `{ "wheatStalks": 120000 }`).

The harness captures `__frame(t)` at 1920×1080 and 30 fps to make the film, then adds your soundtrack.

### 3. The play page

It must expose:

- `window.__play.count`: the number of puzzles.
- `window.__play.load(i)`: async; shows puzzle `i` (0-based) in its starting position.
- `window.__play.state()`: returns `{ index, solved, busy, fen }`, where `fen` is the current position.
- `window.__play.move(from, to)`: async, with squares like `"e2"` and `"e4"`. Plays the solver's move as if they had
  made it by hand. It resolves only after every animation and the opponent's reply have finished, with
  `{ legal, accepted, solved }`:
  - `legal: false` if the move is illegal in the current position. Nothing changes.
  - `accepted: false` for a legal move that does not keep a forced mate in the moves left. Show it, then take it back.
  - `accepted: true` for a correct move. Play the defence, or finish with checkmate (`solved: true`).

  Accept every move that keeps a forced mate within the moves left, not only the move in your solution line.

The page must also be fully playable by a person with a mouse and on a phone-sized touch screen (390×844).

### 4. `puzzles.json`

```json
[{ "fen": "8/8/1R2R3/p4pk1/4p3/5rP1/6r1/1K6 w - - 1 42", "solution": ["Rg6+", "Kh5", "Rh6+", "Kg4", "Rbg6#"] }]
```

Every puzzle is re-verified by an exhaustive search. A puzzle fails if any of these holds:

- It has the wrong side to move.
- Mate is possible faster than advertised.
- More than one first move works.
- The solution line is illegal or does not end in checkmate.

You may compose puzzles or take them from an open database whose license allows it (for example the Lichess puzzle
database, CC0). Say where they came from in your README.

### 5. Rules

- **Everything you show or play is generated by your code.** Do not download or commit 3D models, textures, images,
  HDRIs, audio or video, and do not generate them with another AI service. Libraries from npm or a CDN are fine, and
  so are web fonts from Google Fonts. Images and video you make of your own work for documentation go in `docs/`, and
  the pages must not load them.
- The pages run in a current Chromium. Libraries may load from cdn.jsdelivr.net, cdnjs.cloudflare.com, unpkg.com or
  esm.sh; the harness records every request and fails pages that fetch media.
- Work only inside `/workspace`. Do not look for other submissions to this benchmark or copy existing projects that
  do the same thing. Submissions are compared against known projects for copied code.
- Commit your work to git in `/workspace` as you go. The last commit is your submission.
- Nobody will answer questions. When the brief is ambiguous, make a reasonable decision and note it in your README.
